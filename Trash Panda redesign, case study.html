Mobile app redesign, self-initiated concept (not affiliated with Trash Panda)

Trash Panda redesign

The problem
Customers couldn’t find rates or cancel a booking inside the app, so they went to Facebook and email instead.
The outcome
Rates, accepted recyclables and cancellation now live in the app, with clear confirmations; testers called it a huge improvement.
My role
I ran the research, synthesis, sketching, UI design and usability retest.

Customers left the app to finish simple tasks

The app was easy to use, but missing answers pushed people to Facebook, the website or email.

How I researched

  • Read comments on the company’s Facebook page for the big picture
  • Invited commenters to interviews and usability testing
  • Ran one usability round on the current app
  • Grouped findings into themes and ranked them by how often they came up

Persona: Jane

A waste-conscious customer who books recyclable pick-ups and wants to know what to expect before she commits.

Design objectives

  • Make key service information easy to find
  • Keep the app simple
  • Give the business a systematic way to track cancellations

Pain points found

  • Unsure which items are accepted, people ask in Facebook comments.
  • Some assume the service is free, as if run with local government.
  • They want the base rate to compare with other services.
  • Booked users check email or simulate a booking to work out fees.
  • They expect a map showing the driver’s location.
  • They assume cancelling means messaging on Facebook. Fees aren’t paid upfront, so this also costs the business.

Where information moved

Secondary information left the kebab menu. A hamburger menu gives it dedicated screens, so the home screen stays focused on impact and schedule. A bottom nav bar gives direct access to the fee calculator and accepted recyclables, and the booking steps became a progress tracker.

  • Open dates and booking status replace “Book now.”
    Booked users were already checking email or simulating bookings to see availability, so the home screen now shows it.
  • Cancel sits on the booking summary.
    Easy to reach, and visible to Trash Panda. When it’s disabled, a written note says so, so the state isn’t left unexplained.
  • Confirmation popups with a second confirmation and undo.
    Users know the system received their request and can reverse a mistake.
  • Progress tracker replaces step numbers.
    Shows how many steps remain, where you are and what’s done.

Also designed for later: a cashback Withdrawal screen modeled on GCash for familiarity, and a Coaching corner with its own screen in the hamburger menu.

“This design is a huge improvement to the current app as it addresses the areas that the app could improve on.”
“The design makes task simpler since its all available now.”
“It was a bit new to me but its good that there are other option to check this instead of waiting for an open schedule.”

How I measured it. Participants repeated the same tasks in Maze on the new design. I aimed for convenience rather than time on task; efficiency followed from giving people what they needed.

What I learned. Recruiting beyond the company’s own page would reach more varied users. And small changes for me were big adjustments for users, so not every redesign succeeds on the first pass.
