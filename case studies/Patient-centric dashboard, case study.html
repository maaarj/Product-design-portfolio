Web app dashboard redesign, Theoria Medical

From scheduling dashboard to tactical dashboard

The problem
The physician dashboard showed only visit schedules, not the tasks that needed attention, and physicians had to leave encounter notes to review documents.
The outcome
A task-based dashboard in three groups, with filtered action pages, and flagged vitals and medications to reduce brought into the encounter note.
My role
I joined at brainstorming, researched the pain points, contributed dashboard layout ideas and integrated the workflows into encounter notes.

Physicians kept leaving the note to find what they needed

Theoria Medical offers on-site medical staff and telemedicine. Its physicians use proprietary apps, including a specialized EHR. Care plans depend on labs, medications and medical reports, so that information has to be at hand.

How I researched

  • Gathered information through team channels
  • Watched for emerging themes
  • Retraced a physician’s steps in the app to find pain points

Who it’s for

Physicians who see patients at facilities and must assess, document and sign each encounter.

The challenge

Improve completion of tasks and progress tracking for documents and workflow tasks.

Pain points found

  • The dashboard focused on the physician’s schedule, not facility or organization tasks.
  • Navigating and filtering documents on the medical documents page took multiple steps.
  • During visits, physicians left the encounter notes to check documents or reports.

Mapping where the visit workflow breaks

Dashed boxes show the existing journey. The loops back to “find” steps are where time is lost.

  1. Old dashboardCheck schedule and reason for visit
  2. Patient chartReview records, conditions, nursing and encounter notes
  3. Medical documentsLabs, imaging and more
  4. Encounters pageCreate visit note
  5. Fill out noteFind the encounter note and documents again
  6. Sign and submitClose the encounter

Two loops stand out: finding the encounter note, and going back to find a medical document mid-visit.

Proposed flow

  1. Dashboard widgetTasks grouped by importance
  2. Filtered action pageEverything needing attention
  3. Encounter noteFlagged vitals and meds to reduce inside the note
  4. Sign and submitNo trip back to documents

How tasks are grouped

Tasks fell into three groups, ordered from most to least important: medical documents or reports to be reviewed, schedule-related tasks and progress, and encounter note progress.

Three layouts, one that could scale

We presented three wireframes as final options. Switch between them to compare.

  • Option 1: summary tiles first.
    Tiles for admitted today, discharges, missed visits and meds reduction sit above lists of open encounters, visits and documents.
  • Option 2: task widgets beside the schedule. Chosen.
    Medical documents, diagnostic results, flagged vitals and meds reduction sit with patient schedules and encounters. We pushed for this one because it scales better, and built it around visits and encounters as proof of work.
  • Option 3: alerts above a clinical workflow area.
    Clinical results and flags lead, followed by a large workflow space and open encounters grouped by time.

What we learned. Bringing the workflow into the encounter note presents it as patient-centric data, reachable without leaving the visit.

Next steps. Observe users on the current dashboard to iterate on the schedule-based concept. Then test the proposed dashboard against document completion rate before or during visits, and pinpoint which data belongs in this view.
